How it works

Two beats, two drums

Samba is in 2/4. In a Rio carnival bateria the biggest surdo, the marcação, sounds on the second beat and sets the reference for everyone, while a smaller surdo answers on the first.

Eight squares are two beats split into sixteenths. This is a simplified skeleton: a real bateria has far more going on.

Worth knowing

Surdo

The first surdo, the marcação, is the largest and deepest drum and sounds on the second beat. The second, the resposta, answers on the first. The third, the cutador, plays fills and syncopations across the other two.

The first samba

"Pelo Telefone" was registered with the National Library on 27 November 1916 and released on 78 rpm on 19 January 1917. It is called the founding landmark of samba as a song genre, though the claim of a "first samba" is disputed.

Samba de roda

In 2005 UNESCO declared Samba de Roda part of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. Samba originated in the Afro-Brazilian communities of Bahia and developed in Rio de Janeiro and Salvador.

Forró

Traditional forró (pé-de-serra) is played by an accordion, a triangle and a zabumba drum, in the Northeast of Brazil. Luiz Gonzaga made "Asa Branca" famous across Brazil in the 1940s.

Where "forró" comes from

The main theory is that the word is a clipping of "forrobodó", a big party. The source marks this as unconfirmed.
